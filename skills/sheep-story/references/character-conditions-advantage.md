# Character Conditions and Advantage Model

## Purpose

Use this reference when a character's capabilities, resistances, origin, resources, social position, environment, luck, or exceptional advantages materially shape what choices are available to them.

The core rule is simple:

> Do not confuse what a person **is capable of**, what they **resist**, what they **were born into**, what the **world is doing around them**, and what **chance** tends to do.

These layers can produce similar visible outcomes for completely different reasons. Preserve the cause.

This reference is deliberately not a mandatory point-buy system. Numeric ratings are optional and project-specific. Use the smallest representation that changes story decisions.

## Layer Families

Separate at least these five families when they matter:

```text
1. Intrinsic capability
2. Resistance / tolerance
3. Origin leverage
4. Environmental pressure
5. Narrative contingency
```

Do not collapse them into one generic `power`, `charisma`, `background`, or `luck` score.

## 1. Intrinsic Capability

Intrinsic capability answers:

> What can this person reliably do under ordinary conditions, before equipment, status, allies, or exceptional luck are added?

Possible axes include:

### Physical

- strength
- endurance
- mobility
- coordination
- pain tolerance as a physical dimension
- recovery capacity
- sensory acuity

Do not infer combat skill from physical capacity. A strong civilian and an experienced soldier may have similar bodies but very different action repertoires.

### Cognitive

Keep intelligence decomposed when the distinction matters:

- abstract reasoning
- domain knowledge
- working memory
- pattern recognition
- causal reasoning
- planning
- improvisation
- physical observation
- social observation
- calibration

Use `social-cognitive-profile.md` when cognition and social inference need finer modeling.

### Social Performance

Do not use one omnibus `charisma` value when the mechanism matters. Separate as needed:

- appearance / first-impression response
- presence
- conversational skill
- social observation
- context awareness
- tact
- empathy
- assertiveness
- persuasion / framing
- status fluency
- deception / masking

A beautiful person can be awkward. A plain person can dominate a room. A tactful mediator can have low stage presence. A charismatic speaker can misread the room.

### Special / Setting-Specific Capability

Magic, psychic power, cybernetics, supernatural senses, mutation, divine authority, or other setting-specific abilities belong here only when they are actual capabilities of the person.

Keep power and resistance separate unless the world explicitly defines them as the same mechanism.

## 2. Resistance, Tolerance, and Control

Resistance answers:

> What can act on this person without changing, overwhelming, corrupting, exhausting, frightening, persuading, or disabling them?

Do not assume a positive capability and resistance to that capability are the same axis.

Examples:

```text
high magical power != high magic resistance
high social influence != resistance to manipulation
high intelligence != resistance to propaganda
high pain tolerance != high physical endurance
high courage != low fear response
```

### Willpower Is Not Emotional Regulation

Keep these separable when pressure matters:

#### Willpower / Commitment Persistence

How difficult is it to make the character abandon a chosen goal, principle, promise, or refusal?

A high-will character may still cry, panic, shake, or become visibly overwhelmed while continuing forward.

#### Emotional Regulation

How well can the character modulate, suppress, redirect, or recover from emotional expression in the moment?

A low-will, high-regulation character may look perfectly composed while quietly giving up or changing sides.

#### Stress Tolerance

How much does performance degrade under prolonged pressure, sleep loss, overload, uncertainty, fear, pain, or repeated setbacks?

#### Belief Resistance

How resistant is a belief or identity commitment to persuasion, propaganda, social proof, intimidation, anomalous evidence, or ideological pressure?

Belief resistance can be a strength or a liability. Strong resistance can protect against manipulation and also make updating harder.

#### Corruption / Influence Resistance

Use a setting-specific axis when supernatural, technological, memetic, biological, or institutional influence has distinct mechanics.

### Courage Is Not Fearlessness

Prefer:

```text
fear response
+ action under fear
+ regulation
+ commitment
```

over a single `courage` label when the scene depends on the distinction.

## 3. Origin Leverage

Origin leverage answers:

> What advantages, constraints, debts, expectations, networks, enemies, and fallback resources exist before this character makes their first independent choice?

Possible dimensions:

- family wealth
- family status
- political access
- education access
- professional inheritance
- social network
- legal protection
- inherited reputation
- inherited enemies
- inherited obligations
- cultural capital
- safety net
- citizenship / caste / class standing
- patronage
- debt
- stigma

### Resource Is Not Personality

Do not infer personality directly from origin.

```text
wealthy != arrogant
poor != humble
noble != charismatic
outlaw family != antisocial
elite education != intelligent
```

Origin changes available options and repeated experiences. Those experiences may shape personality over time, but the causal chain should be shown or established.

### Same Ability, Different Leverage

Two equally capable characters can face radically different consequences:

```text
same mistake
+ powerful family safety net
→ private repair, second chance, reputation management

same mistake
+ stigmatized family / no protection
→ arrest, exclusion, loss of livelihood
```

This difference can create divergent risk tolerance, institutional trust, ambition, resentment, or survival strategies without treating those adaptations as genetically attached to class.

## 4. Environmental Pressure

Environmental pressure answers:

> What does the surrounding world routinely demand, permit, punish, threaten, or make scarce?

Possible dimensions:

- physical safety
- war pressure
- crime / predation
- scarcity
- disease burden
- pollution / environmental hazard
- political stability
- institutional brutality
- surveillance
- corruption
- religious / ideological enforcement
- social mobility
- discrimination
- education availability
- technology access
- healthcare access
- travel freedom
- disaster frequency
- community cohesion

### Environment Is Not Character

A character born into a war zone is not automatically violent, traumatized, cynical, or competent.

Environment creates pressures and exposures. Character response depends on:

```text
temperament
+ capability
+ relationships
+ resources
+ luck
+ meaning-making
+ duration
```

Record learned adaptations only when evidence supports them.

### Starting Environment vs Current Environment

Keep origin environment and current environment separate.

A person raised in abundance but currently stranded in famine will carry different assumptions from someone raised under chronic scarcity.

### Environment as Story Engine

An environment value is useful only if it changes:

- what risks are ordinary
- what resources are scarce
- what institutions can be trusted
- what behavior is rewarded
- what information is available
- how expensive recovery is
- what counts as normal

## 5. Narrative Contingency / Luck

Luck is not an ordinary capability. Treat it as a project-level or diegetic rule only when the story actually uses chance asymmetrically.

Possible interpretations:

- literal supernatural luck
- probability manipulation
- genre-level coincidence bias
- recurring bad-luck comic engine
- fate / destiny system
- statistical fortune without supernatural cause

### Luck Must Not Become Plot Armor

Good luck should usually change options rather than guarantee outcomes.

Example:

```text
weapon jams and saves the character from immediate death
→ now they must escape unarmed
```

Bad luck should usually create complications rather than force arbitrary failure.

Example:

```text
last train departs early
→ character must take a dangerous route
```

Avoid:

```text
Luck high → every crisis resolves itself
Luck low → every attempt fails regardless of competence
```

### Contingency Audit

For any unusually lucky or unlucky event, ask:

```markdown
What random variable changed?
Why is this within the project's luck contract?
What new option or complication appears?
What still depends on the character's decisions?
Would the scene still contain meaningful agency without the coincidence?
```

Use `reader-promise-and-tone.md` when luck is part of a comedy, tragedy, fate, or genre contract.

## Derived Consequences

The most important rule is:

> Store causes, then derive consequences. Do not store consequences as if they were universal traits.

A visible behavior such as `NPCs keep their distance` may come from:

- threatening body language
- poor hygiene
- feared reputation
- high social status
- contagious disease
- supernatural aura
- legal stigma
- known violence
- deliberate intimidation

The output is similar. The story meaning is not.

Use this shape when a condition repeatedly affects scenes:

```markdown
Source condition:
Mechanism:
Who can perceive it:
Typical consequence:
Exceptions:
What can change the consequence:
```

## Capability + Resource + Environment

Actual performance often depends on all three:

```text
intrinsic capability
× available resources
× environmental affordance
× current state
× relationship / institutional access
```

Do not let an intrinsic stat magically create tools, credentials, money, allies, or legal authority.

A brilliant engineer without parts may still be unable to repair the engine. A mediocre official with authority and a large staff may accomplish more than a genius outsider.

## Advantage Budget

The Advantage Budget is a narrative diagnostic, not a mandatory numerical balance rule.

Use it when a character accumulates many exceptional benefits:

- exceptional capability
- exceptional beauty / presence
- elite status
- wealth
- broad network
- rare powers
- strong resistances
- extraordinary luck
- unusually safe origin

Ask:

```markdown
Which advantages materially change options?
Which advantages overlap and therefore add little new capability?
Which advantages attract attention, obligation, responsibility, exploitation, or stronger opposition?
Which advantages consume scarce time, secrecy, trust, or resources?
Where does meaningful friction come from if the character can solve ordinary problems easily?
Does the project's reader promise intentionally support low-friction power fantasy?
```

### Do Not Force Artificial Weaknesses

Reject the pattern:

```text
extreme genius
+ extreme wealth
+ extreme charisma
+ extreme combat ability
→ balancing flaw: cannot cook / afraid of insects
```

A weakness matters only if it affects consequential decisions, relationships, costs, or vulnerability.

### Counterpressure, Not Punishment

Exceptional advantages often create natural counterpressure:

```text
high status
→ obligations, visibility, political enemies

high charisma
→ attention, dependency, cult of personality, manipulation attempts

high intelligence
→ responsibility, isolation, overconfidence, harder problems

rare power
→ exploitation, surveillance, scarcity, corruption risk

extreme willpower
→ rigidity, refusal to retreat, difficulty compromising

wealth
→ succession conflict, kidnapping risk, obligations, distorted feedback
```

These are possibilities, not automatic penalties. Use only consequences supported by the setting and character.

### Power Fantasy Exception

If the reader promise explicitly wants an overwhelmingly advantaged protagonist, do not secretly sabotage the premise in the name of balance.

Instead place friction in domains the advantages do not erase:

- scale
- uncertainty
- conflicting values
- responsibility
- irreversible tradeoffs
- relationships
- time
- political legitimacy
- coordination
- meaning

## Advantage Conversion Rule

A strong advantage becomes story-useful when it creates at least one of:

- a new option
- a faster solution
- a unique responsibility
- a new kind of opposition
- a social expectation
- an information problem
- a maintenance cost
- a moral tradeoff
- a visibility problem
- a relationship distortion

If it changes none of these, it is mostly decorative.

## Negative Conditions and "Refunds"

When importing a point-buy or game-like character system, do not assume negative conditions are free narrative currency.

A disadvantage should not exist only to purchase unrelated strengths.

Ask:

```markdown
Does the disadvantage actually constrain scenes?
Who experiences the cost?
Can money, allies, equipment, status, or preparation neutralize it?
Is it temporary, structural, social, physical, or environmental?
Does the claimed severity match its actual consequences?
```

A severe disadvantage that never appears in choices is not meaningful balance.

## Origin Does Not Equal Destiny

Avoid deterministic chains such as:

```text
violent childhood → violent adult
poverty → criminality
wealth → arrogance
war zone → trauma stereotype
elite family → competence
```

Prefer:

```text
origin pressure
→ repeated experiences
→ coping / learned adaptation
→ current belief or behavior
```

Different characters can adapt to the same environment in opposite ways.

## Review Failure Modes

Flag these:

### Omnibus Stat

One value such as `charisma`, `intelligence`, or `will` silently controls unrelated behaviors.

### Resource-from-Ability

The character's intelligence or charm conjures equipment, money, authority, contacts, or credentials never established.

### Environment-as-Personality

The narration treats poverty, war, class, or family history as a personality trait without showing adaptation.

### Resistance Collapse

High power is assumed to imply immunity to the same power or to unrelated forms of influence.

### Plot-Armor Luck

Coincidence repeatedly removes consequences instead of changing available options.

### Cosmetic Disadvantage

A severe listed weakness never matters when the story tests it.

### Fake Balance

An overwhelmingly advantaged character receives an irrelevant quirk labeled as their balancing flaw.

### Punitive Balance

The story invents arbitrary suffering solely because the character is powerful, violating the reader promise.

### Starting-State Determinism

Origin is treated as destiny rather than pressure filtered through experience and choice.

## Principle

A character is not only a personality inside a body.

They are a person with capabilities and resistances, born into unequal leverage, acting inside an environment that opens and closes options, while chance may occasionally bend the path.

Keep those causes separate so the story can show how they interact.
