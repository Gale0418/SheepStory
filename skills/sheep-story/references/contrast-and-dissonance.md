# Character Contrast and Dissonance

## Purpose

Use this reference when a character's appeal, tension, comedy, mystery, or tragedy depends on a meaningful gap between expectation and reality, public and private behavior, words and actions, values and conduct, or self-image and actual motive.

This includes:

- gap appeal / gap moe
- public-private contrast
- unexpectedly soft, silly, competent, frightening, or vulnerable sides
- saying one thing while doing another
- indirect affection and defensive denial
- hypocrisy
- strategic deception
- self-deception
- value-action conflict
- unreliable self-description

Do not turn every contradiction into a social mask or every word-action gap into romance. First identify the mechanism.

## Core Distinction

**Contrast** is a structure in the character.

**Gap appeal** is an audience response to discovering that structure.

A stern person quietly repairing a child's toy may create gap appeal because the act violates the audience's established expectation while remaining consistent with a deeper value. The same action from a consistently gentle character is still kind, but it does not produce the same contrast effect.

## Contrast Requires a Baseline

Before using a contrast beat, define:

```markdown
Expected surface:
How that expectation was established:
Contrasting side:
Why both sides belong to the same person:
Who can see each side:
Trigger or access condition:
Cost or vulnerability of revealing it:
What changes after discovery:
```

Without a clear baseline, the second side is not a reveal. It is merely another random trait.

## Contrast Types

### Affect Gap

A cold, severe, frightening, or restrained person reveals warmth, tenderness, delight, embarrassment, or grief.

Potential mechanism:

- selective trust
- professional restraint
- shame around vulnerability
- a private attachment
- emotional inexperience
- deliberate intimidation

### Competence Gap

A clumsy, ordinary, comic, lazy, timid, or distracted person demonstrates exceptional skill in a specific domain.

Keep actual capability and displayed capability separate. The competence may be hidden deliberately, overlooked by others, or activated only under pressure.

### Role-Hobby Gap

A person's role, appearance, or reputation contrasts with a harmless preference, ritual, craft, or fascination.

Examples:

- feared commander who makes tiny pastries
- elegant diplomat obsessed with cheap monster movies
- grim undertaker who loves loud dance music

The hobby should connect to need, history, stress relief, community, or identity when it matters repeatedly. A random cute object is not automatically depth.

### Public-Private Gap

The character performs one mode in public and another in private.

Possible causes:

- status expectations
- safety
- reputation management
- caregiving burden
- professional role
- family culture
- deception
- exhaustion after performance

### Threat Gap

A harmless or forgettable surface conceals danger, influence, authority, or strategic competence; or a frightening surface conceals low threat and insecurity.

Use `social-cognitive-profile.md` for masking and competence display.

### Moral Gap

A person known for kindness makes a cruel choice, or a villain shows a real boundary, loyalty, or mercy.

Do not mistake one pleasant act for redemption or one failure for total corruption. Track patterns, costs, and consequences.

### Scale Gap

The character's concerns operate at two wildly different scales:

- saves a city but panics over a phone call
- commands a fleet but cannot cook rice
- understands cosmology but misreads a date invitation

This is especially useful for separating intelligence, competence, and social ability.

## Words, Actions, and Body Are Separate Channels

For important moments, track:

```markdown
What the character says:
What they do:
What their body leaks or suppresses:
What they believe they are doing:
What the listener infers:
What the reader can infer:
```

These channels may align or diverge.

Example:

```text
Words: "I do not care whether you come back."
Action: leaves a light on and repairs the travel cloak.
Body: does not look at the door when footsteps pass.
Self-story: "I am only being practical."
```

Do not automatically translate every spoken sentence into its opposite. The action may reveal care, guilt, habit, control, fear, or obligation rather than hidden romance.

## Word-Action Gap Taxonomy

### Defensive Indirectness

The character feels something but cannot state it directly because of pride, shame, fear of rejection, role expectations, or emotional inexperience.

Common forms:

- criticism used to deliver care
- practical help instead of reassurance
- denial paired with protective action
- joking instead of confession

This can support tsundere, kuudere, or other indirect-affection patterns, but do not reduce those characters to an automatic opposite-language decoder.

### Protective Lie

The character says something false to protect another person, conceal danger, preserve dignity, or absorb blame.

The lie should have a cost and a plausible reason that direct truth feels worse.

### Politeness or Role Performance

The character says the socially required thing while privately disagreeing or feeling something else.

This is not necessarily deception in a villainous sense. It may be professionalism, diplomacy, survival, or emotional labor.

### Strategic Deception

Words and actions diverge because the character wants another person to form a false model.

Track:

- intended belief
- evidence planted
- behavior suppressed
- exit strategy
- verification pressure
- leak pattern

Use Mask Dynamics in `social-cognitive-profile.md` for sustained deception.

### Hypocrisy

The character publicly endorses a value while repeatedly exempting their own behavior without honest acknowledgment.

Distinguish hypocrisy from:

- one failure followed by remorse
- unavoidable compromise
- ignorance of consequences
- a value conflict the character openly admits
- coercion

Hypocrisy becomes dramatically useful when the character's self-justification affects trust, power, or downfall.

### Self-Deception

The character sincerely believes a flattering explanation of their own behavior.

```text
Claimed motive: "I am protecting her."
Observed pattern: controlling her choices and punishing independence.
Hidden need: fear of abandonment.
```

The character may not be consciously lying. This requires separating public speech, private belief, and underlying drive.

### Value-Action Conflict

Two genuine values cannot both be honored under current conditions.

```text
loyalty to friend
vs
responsibility to civilians
```

The resulting inconsistency can be tragic without making the character dishonest.

### Weakness Against Principle

The character believes a value, intends to follow it, and fails under temptation, fear, fatigue, addiction, attachment, or pressure.

The story should preserve the difference between "does not believe" and "could not live up to belief."

### Unreliable Self-Narration

The character describes themselves inaccurately because of limited insight, bias, trauma, ideology, memory distortion, or deliberate narration.

Use `character-memory-belief.md` to track the difference between canonical behavior and the character's account.

## Designing Gap Appeal

A useful gap beat often follows this sequence:

```text
1. Establish a legible expectation.
2. Hint at an incompatible detail.
3. Reveal the contrasting behavior under a specific trigger.
4. Let another character or the reader register the mismatch.
5. Preserve some cost, privacy, or vulnerability.
6. Update the relationship or interpretation.
```

### Access Conditions

The contrasting side may appear:

- only around a trusted person
- only when no audience is present
- when a specific hobby or subject appears
- under emergency pressure
- when caring for someone weaker
- after status or authority is removed
- when the character believes nobody is watching

Do not make the soft side appear only because the user arrived unless the relationship has earned that access.

### Reveal Size

Small reveals are often stronger than complete reversals:

- one softened word
- a private object
- a habitual task done carefully
- a brief loss of composure
- an unexpectedly precise kindness
- a laugh the character immediately suppresses

The goal is not always to replace the baseline. The contrast can remain situational.

## Contrast and Relationship Progression

Track who has access to which side:

```markdown
Public sees:
Coworkers see:
Friends see:
Family sees:
Specific person sees:
Reader sees before the cast:
What access must be earned:
What exposure would feel violating:
```

This prevents "private side" from becoming the default voice after one scene.

## Contrast as Plot Mechanism

Contrast can do more than create cuteness:

- expose a hidden allegiance
- reveal a false reputation
- create mistaken identity
- provide a clue
- change threat assessment
- make a betrayal hurt more
- show recovery or deterioration
- turn comedy into dread
- let a villain's genuine affection become a constraint

A contrast beat should alter interpretation, relationship, risk, or choice when it carries major narrative weight.

## Contrast Versus Inconsistency

A convincing contrast has a shared causal root.

```text
Surface severity + private gentleness
shared root: intense responsibility and fear of failing dependents
```

An arbitrary inconsistency has no bridge:

```text
The character is cruel for ten scenes, then suddenly loves puppies solely because the author wants a cute illustration.
```

Ask:

- What need or history produces both modes?
- What situation selects one mode over the other?
- What does each mode protect?
- What cost accompanies switching?

## Failure Modes

### Contrast Saturation

If every scene reveals a new opposite trait, the character stops having a baseline.

### Mechanical Reversal

```text
"I hate you" always means "I love you."
```

This destroys ambiguity and turns social inference into substitution.

### Unearned Exclusivity

The character is hostile to everyone but instantly soft only for the protagonist without relationship cause.

### Abuse Laundering

A cute private side does not erase coercion, cruelty, stalking, humiliation, or violence. Preserve consequences and other characters' boundaries.

### Cosmetic Quirk

A contrast item or hobby appears but never affects behavior, relationship, or interpretation.

### Random Contradiction

Opposing traits appear according to scene convenience rather than trigger, state, relationship, or motive.

### Total Mask Collapse

After one reveal, the public persona disappears permanently even though the social conditions that created it remain.

## Review Pass

- Is the baseline established before the contrast?
- Are both sides causally compatible?
- Does the trigger fit the character and relationship?
- Is the audience meant to find the gap cute, frightening, tragic, or suspicious?
- Does the action contradict the words in a specific way rather than simply reverse them?
- Does the character know they are being inconsistent?
- Are hypocrisy, weakness, compromise, self-deception, and strategic deception distinguished?
- Does the reveal update anything?
- Is the gap being repeated so often that it becomes the new baseline?
- Are harmful actions still allowed to have consequences?

## Principle

A strong contrast does not make a character two unrelated people. It reveals that one legible surface was never the whole causal system. Let words, actions, body, belief, and audience expectation diverge for a reason—and make the discovery change how the character is understood.
