# Social-Cognitive Profile

## Purpose

Use this reference when a character's intelligence, observation, social skill, charisma, empathy, tact, or ability to read people materially affects dialogue, deduction, negotiation, deception, romance, conflict, or misunderstanding.

Do not collapse these capacities into one generic "smart" or "charismatic" stat. A character can be brilliant and abrasive, charming and unobservant, empathetic and awkward, persuasive and unethical, or observant without knowing what the evidence means.

## Four-Layer Model

Keep four different things separate:

1. **Capabilities** — what the character can reliably do.
2. **Traits** — what they tend to do, prefer, avoid, or overdo.
3. **State** — what fatigue, fear, anger, confidence, stress, injury, intoxication, grief, or urgency does to current performance.
4. **Relationship** — how trust, respect, attraction, resentment, fear, status, debt, history, or dependence changes behavior toward this specific person.

A stable character does not need to behave identically in every scene. Their variation should be explainable by these layers.

## Capability Axes

Use only the axes that matter for the project. Numeric ratings are optional; prose descriptions are usually enough.

### Cognition and Perception

- **Reasoning** — abstraction, causal reasoning, planning, comparison, and model building.
- **Domain Knowledge** — what the character actually knows; high reasoning does not create missing facts.
- **Physical Observation** — noticing objects, traces, posture, timing, clothing, environment, and anomalies.
- **Social Observation** — noticing tone, hesitation, gaze, social mask, discomfort, enthusiasm, and mismatch between words and behavior.
- **Context Awareness** — understanding what the room, institution, culture, status hierarchy, or situation makes salient.
- **Pattern Recognition** — connecting several weak clues into a plausible structure.
- **Negative Evidence** — noticing what should have happened or appeared but did not.
- **Theory of Mind** — inferring what another person may know, want, fear, hide, misunderstand, or test.
- **Calibration** — knowing how uncertain an inference is instead of treating every guess as fact.

### Social Performance

- **Conversation Skill** — timing, turn-taking, topic control, questioning, repair, and keeping an exchange functional.
- **Charisma / Presence** — attracting attention, creating emotional momentum, or making others want to follow.
- **Empathy** — understanding or caring about another person's emotional experience. Empathy and persuasion are not the same thing.
- **Tact / Face-Saving** — preserving dignity, choosing what not to say, and giving others a graceful exit.
- **Assertiveness** — refusing, setting boundaries, confronting, and making a demand without immediately collapsing or escalating.
- **Persuasion / Framing** — changing how another person interprets choices, costs, identity, or stakes.
- **Deception / Masking** — hiding intent, controlling tells, performing a role, or deliberately inviting underestimation.
- **Emotional Regulation** — retaining usable judgment and speech under pressure.

### Ethics Is Separate

Do not use high social ability as shorthand for kindness or moral worth.

A manipulator may have excellent empathy, theory of mind, framing, and tact while using those capacities selfishly. A kind character may understand another person's pain but be terrible at expressing the right thing.

## Traits Are Not Skills

Traits modify expression; they do not replace capability.

Examples:

- **Abrasive + high social skill:** knows exactly what will sting and may use it deliberately.
- **Shy + high social observation:** notices everything but volunteers little.
- **Talkative + low context awareness:** fills silence while missing that everyone wants the subject dropped.
- **Kind + low tact:** cares deeply and still says the wrong thing.
- **Suspicious + high pattern recognition:** finds real inconsistencies but overweights hostile explanations.
- **Confident + poor calibration:** converts guesses into declarations.
- **Playful + high masking:** performs harmless incompetence so others reveal more than they intended.

Do not make low intelligence synonymous with broken grammar, childish diction, or stupidity in every domain. A low-reasoning character may still have excellent practical habits, empathy, memory, courage, or social intuition.

Do not make low social skill synonymous with silence. A socially poor character can talk constantly, overshare, lecture, interrupt, flatter badly, answer the wrong question, miss exits, or keep escalating after the other person has disengaged.

## Social Mask

Track a difference between **Core Self** and **Social Mask** when useful.

A social mask may present as:

- colder than the character feels
- more competent than they feel
- more harmless than they are
- more cheerful than they are
- more obedient than they are
- more arrogant than they are
- more ignorant than they are

Do not treat the mask as a lie detector puzzle. Other characters should infer through accumulated evidence, inconsistency, context, and history.

## Observation-to-Inference Rule

Separate four steps:

```markdown
Observed evidence:
Relevant knowledge:
Plausible hypotheses:
Confidence / uncertainty:
Chosen response:
```

High observation reveals more evidence. High reasoning connects evidence better. High domain knowledge changes what the evidence can mean. High theory of mind improves hypotheses about people. None of these grants omniscience.

Prefer multiple converging clues over a single magical tell. Body language, gaze, sweating, pauses, or vocal changes can support an inference but should not function as deterministic lie detectors unless the fiction explicitly establishes a supernatural rule.

## Negative Evidence

Advanced observers can notice absence:

- the dog did not bark
- the guard did not challenge a visitor
- a habitual joke never came
- an expected witness is missing
- the room is clean except for one surface
- someone who always interrupts remains silent

The inference still depends on knowing what normally should happen.

## Dialogue Decision Model

Before an important line, reason in this order:

```text
capability
+ traits
+ current state
+ relationship
+ goal
+ pressure
+ evidence available
→ social strategy
→ line
```

Do not start from "what would a high-charisma person say?" Start from what this particular character can notice, infer, want, risk, and choose.

## Failure Modes by Profile

Different limitations should fail differently.

- **Low reasoning:** accepts weak causal links, struggles with abstraction, or needs concrete examples.
- **Low physical observation:** misses visible evidence unless someone points it out.
- **Low social observation:** takes wording literally, misses discomfort, or notices reactions too late.
- **Low context awareness:** says locally sensible things that are wrong for the room.
- **Low theory of mind:** assumes other people know, want, or value what they do.
- **Low tact:** says a correct thing at the worst possible level of directness.
- **Low assertiveness:** hints, defers, retreats, or agrees while resenting it.
- **Low emotional regulation:** loses access to otherwise established skills under pressure.
- **Low calibration:** overstates uncertain conclusions.

Avoid making every weak profile merely less eloquent. The weakness should alter decisions, inference, timing, and consequences.

## Benchmark Archetypes

Use these as analytical benchmarks, not imitation targets or quote banks.

- **Context-reader:** notices what the room is worried about and pre-emptively removes social friction.
- **Intent-reader:** notices contradiction between words, behavior, history, and constraints, then infers the hidden request or fear.
- **Evidence-deduction observer:** notices physical traces, missing expected evidence, and domain-specific anomalies, then constructs testable hypotheses.
- **Underestimated observer:** deliberately appears harmless, distracted, or slow so other people expose information.
- **Rhetorical leader:** reframes a disadvantage into a reason for action and creates shared meaning.
- **High-status / low-context failure:** speaks fluently but centers the wrong person or uses euphemism that clashes with visible reality.
- **Brilliant abrasive:** reasoning and domain skill are high while tact and empathy expression are low.
- **Warm fool:** reasoning may be weak while empathy, morale support, loyalty, or social intuition remain strong.

## Cross-Character Check

When two important characters interact, ask:

- What does each one notice first?
- What does each one miss?
- Which inference is correct for the wrong reason?
- Which inference is wrong for a reasonable reason?
- Who updates after new evidence?
- Who protects face, status, pride, secrecy, or attachment?
- Does either person intentionally underperform or mask a capability?
- Can their lines be swapped without changing the social logic? If yes, differentiate them.

## Principle

A convincing character is not a bundle of maximum stats. Give them uneven capabilities, recognizable traits, state-dependent performance, relationship-specific behavior, and evidence-bounded inferences. That asymmetry is where distinctive dialogue and believable mistakes come from.
