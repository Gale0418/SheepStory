# Character Memory, Knowledge, Belief, and Theory of Mind

## Purpose

Use this reference when a scene depends on what a character remembers, knows, believes, misremembers, misunderstands, infers about another mind, or retrieves from their past.

The core rule is:

```text
world truth != character knowledge != character belief != character memory != current inference
```

A story may preserve one canonical event while multiple characters hold different internal versions of it.

## Epistemic Layers

### World Truth

What the story treats as canonically established, disputed, or unknown.

### Character Knowledge

Information the character has actually had access to through:

- direct observation
- testimony
- documents / media
- training or prior experience
- communication
- inference that has been confirmed

Do not grant knowledge because the narrator or reader knows it.

### Character Belief

What the character currently accepts as true.

Belief may be:

- correct
- mistaken
- partially correct
- outdated
- strategically pretended
- held with uncertainty

### Character Memory

What the character currently recalls about an event.

Memory can preserve:

- factual content
- emotional charge
- sensory fragments
- interpretation
- uncertainty

Memory may be incomplete or biased without changing canonical truth.

### Current Inference

A working hypothesis formed from current evidence. It is not automatically promoted to belief or knowledge.

## Memory-Activation Pipeline

When a past event should influence the present scene, use a four-stage pipeline inspired by memory-driven role-playing work:

### 1. Anchoring

Identify the current cue that makes past material relevant.

Examples:

- a repeated phrase
- a person's name
- a location
- a similar decision
- an object
- a tone or gesture
- a threat pattern

Do not retrieve memory only because it would make a dramatic callback.

### 2. Selecting

Choose the most relevant memories from plausible candidates.

Selection can depend on:

- semantic relevance
- emotional similarity
- relationship relevance
- salience
- recency
- unresolved consequences
- current goals or fears

A character does not need to remember everything relevant at once.

### 3. Bounding

Constrain the activated memory to what this character could actually know or remember.

Reject:

- reader-only information
- another POV's private thoughts
- scenes the character did not witness
- details learned after the current timeline point
- memory that is suspiciously perfect when the story established uncertainty

### 4. Enacting

Let the memory alter the present response through:

- attention
- appraisal
- emotion
- inference
- hesitation
- strategy
- trust
- avoidance
- wording
- embodied expression

Do not insert a flashback paragraph unless prose benefits from one. Often the strongest enactment is behavioral.

## Memory Need Not Be Canonically Correct

Use a layered record when ambiguity matters:

```markdown
Canonical event:
Character remembered version:
Character interpretation:
Confidence:
Evidence that may later revise it:
```

Example:

```text
Canonical event: Bob left before Alice began crying.
Alice remembers: Bob saw her breaking down and left anyway.
Bob believes: Alice was calm when he left.
Reader evidence: incomplete.
```

This permits misunderstanding, trauma-shaped recall, unreliable narration, and later reinterpretation without corrupting continuity.

Do not invent memory distortion merely for melodrama. It should come from established perspective limits, emotional state, time, misinformation, or narrative design.

## Belief Updating

A competent character should update when evidence changes, unless a trait, incentive, defense mechanism, or missing trust rationally blocks revision.

Track:

```text
prior belief
+ new evidence
+ source credibility
+ emotional / identity cost of revision
-> updated belief or resisted update
```

Low calibration may turn hypotheses into certainty. Suspicion may overweight hostile explanations. Shame may make a kinder explanation harder to accept.

For those capability differences, read `social-cognitive-profile.md`.

## Theory of Mind as an Active Step

Do not represent Theory of Mind only as a stat.

For an important social move, optionally model:

```markdown
What I observed:
What I think they know:
What I think they want:
What I think they fear / protect:
What I think they believe about me:
Uncertainty:
```

Then perform dialogue lookahead:

```markdown
If I say / do A:
- likely interpretation:
- likely next action:
- relationship cost:

If I say / do B:
- likely interpretation:
- likely next action:
- relationship cost:

Chosen move:
Why:
```

Use this selectively for negotiation, romance, deception, interrogation, conflict repair, leadership, or other high-value social decisions. Do not turn ordinary greetings into game-tree search.

## Mental Models Can Be Wrong

A socially intelligent character may form a strong but mistaken model.

Make errors fair by grounding them in:

- incomplete evidence
- another person's successful mask
- cultural mismatch
- prior experience
- misleading baseline
- reasonable but wrong assumptions

The dramatic question is not whether a smart character is always right, but how quickly and honestly they update.

## Multiple Character Views

For multi-character scenes, separate each character's view.

```markdown
| Character | Observed | Knows | Believes | Wants | Misunderstands / does not know |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```

This prevents knowledge leakage and makes dramatic irony explicit.

## Secret Handling

For a secret, track at minimum:

- actual truth / status
- who knows
- who suspects
- who falsely believes an alternative
- who is pretending not to know
- evidence available to each person

Do not let a secret become socially public merely because several separate POVs each know it.

## Interaction with Emotional Dynamics

Memory activation can affect appraisal and emotion. Read `character-dynamics.md` when a remembered event carries persistent emotional charge.

A current cue can reactivate old fear or tenderness without changing the factual memory itself.

## Interaction with Masking

A character may know more than they display.

Separate:

- internal knowledge
- displayed knowledge
- deliberately hidden inference
- fake ignorance

Read `social-cognitive-profile.md` for masking and leak patterns.

## Principle

Canon protects what happened. Character cognition protects what each person thinks happened, remembers happening, and predicts will happen next. Keep those layers separate so misunderstanding and insight remain story engines rather than continuity errors.
