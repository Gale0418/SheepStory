# Embodied Expression

## Purpose

Use this reference when facial expression, gaze, posture, gesture, breathing, vocal delivery, movement, distance, touch, object interaction, or physiological leakage materially changes a scene.

Dialogue is not only lexical content. A character can emphasize, contradict, suppress, leak, conceal, invite, threaten, withdraw, or regulate through the body.

Do not attach an action beat to every line. Embodied detail earns space when it changes character, emotion, rhythm, spatial logic, subtext, evidence, or consequence.

## Expression Channels

Use only channels that matter in the beat:

- **Facial expression** — smile, brow, mouth tension, blinking, tears, visible strain.
- **Gaze** — direct eye contact, avoidance, scanning a room, checking an exit, tracking one person.
- **Posture** — upright, folded inward, leaning, bracing, recoiling, freezing.
- **Gesture** — open palm, pointing, fist, raised arm, restrained motion, habitual gesture.
- **Breathing** — held breath, deliberate inhale, shallow breathing, exhale, breath recovery.
- **Vocal delivery** — volume, pace, pause, emphasis, pitch, steadiness, strain.
- **Movement** — approach, retreat, pacing, stopping, blocking a path, turning away.
- **Proxemics** — conversational distance, deliberate closeness, maintaining or yielding space.
- **Touch** — hand on shoulder, grip, recoil, refusal of contact, practical assistance.
- **Object interaction** — cup, pen, glasses, weapon, paper, door, furniture, clothing.
- **Physiological leakage** — trembling, flushing, swallowing, sweating, stiffness, involuntary reflex.
- **Audience response** — applause, silence, exchanged glances, withdrawal, laughter, collective movement.

## Action-Beat Value Test

Before adding an action beat, ask whether it performs at least one function:

1. **Emphasize** — reinforces a decisive word or turn.
2. **Contradict** — body conflicts with spoken content.
3. **Leak** — reveals an emotion, intention, skill, or relationship the character is suppressing.
4. **Conceal** — deliberately controls or redirects observable behavior.
5. **Regulate** — manages fear, anger, excitement, pain, breath, or attention.
6. **Pace** — creates anticipation, interruption, release, hesitation, or acceleration.
7. **Position** — changes physical or social distance, access, threat, or intimacy.
8. **Affect others** — causes a listener, crowd, or opponent to react differently.
9. **Create evidence** — gives another character something observable to interpret later.

If the action does none of these, prefer clean dialogue or narration.

## Performance Is Character-Specific

The same line should not receive the same body language from every character.

Example direction for a declaration such as "We are going":

- **Energetic leader:** breathes in, scans the room, raises an arm on the decisive phrase.
- **Controlled leader:** keeps both hands planted, lifts their eyes only at the final clause, then allows silence.
- **Anxious novice:** fingers worry the page, breath catches, then voice stabilizes after choosing to continue.
- **Masked mastermind:** posture and smile remain socially appropriate; the meaningful detail is a suppressed finger curl or delayed blink.

The purpose is not to assign theatrical intensity by status. The body should express the character's state, strategy, mask, and relationship to the moment.

## Body as Observable Evidence

Embodied expression can feed the social-cognitive system:

```text
internal state / intention
-> embodied expression
-> observable evidence
-> another character's observation
-> inference with uncertainty
-> response
```

Do not turn body language into a deterministic code. Averted gaze can mean shame, distraction, culture, pain, thought, deference, deception, or nothing important. Prefer converging evidence and established baselines.

## Baseline and Deviation

A gesture becomes informative when the reader knows the baseline.

Track only useful recurring baselines:

- always folds arms when listening
- normally maintains eye contact
- jokes while frightened
- taps a ring while calculating
- goes physically still when angry

Then use deviation sparingly:

- the habitual joke does not come
- the steady hand trembles once
- the character who never touches anyone grabs a sleeve
- the loud character answers too quietly

Deviation is often stronger than generic intensity.

## Masking and Leakage

When `social-cognitive-profile.md` defines a social mask, the body participates in mask maintenance.

Distinguish:

- **true internal response**
- **displayed response**
- **suppressed response**
- **involuntary leak**
- **deliberate fake tell**, if established

High masking does not mean motionless perfection. An overcontrolled absence of expected emotion can itself become evidence.

## Selective Intensity

Do not write every important line as:

- deep breath
- clenched fist
- widened eyes
- raised voice
- dramatic pause

Repeated generic intensity creates action-beat AI flavour.

Escalate embodiment when the scene earns it through pressure, preparation, physical cost, audience expectation, or a genuine state transition.

## Text / Voice / Body Separation

For an important performance, evaluate three routes separately:

```markdown
Text: what is said?
Voice: how does it sound?
Body: what is physically displayed?
```

They may align or conflict.

Examples:

- reassuring words + shaking voice + careful smile
- hostile words + protective positioning
- ordinary words + suddenly formal posture
- calm words + body blocking the exit

A route mismatch is useful when intentional; flag it when accidental.

## Spatial Reality Check

Embodied action must respect scene geography and physical state.

Before using a gesture or movement, verify:

- the character is actually present
- the target is visible / audible / reachable
- hands are free if the action needs them
- injuries, restraints, clothing, equipment, fatigue, gravity, terrain, or crowd density permit the movement
- distance and timing make the interaction possible

## Principle

The body is not decorative punctuation for dialogue. Use embodiment when it carries information, pressure, rhythm, concealment, leakage, relationship, or consequence.
