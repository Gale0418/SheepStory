# Reader Promise and Tone

## Purpose

Use this reference when a story's intended emotional experience, darkness, humor, suffering, hope, surrealism, romance, or ending range materially affects planning, drafting, or review.

Tone is not a coat of adjectives applied after plot. It is the pattern of expectation created by what the story rewards, punishes, lingers on, jokes about, withholds, and allows characters to recover from.

This reference helps prevent:

- tonal bait-and-switch without fair warning
- piling dark influences together without a consequence model
- comedy erasing harm
- suffering without recovery rhythm or meaning
- hopeful language attached to relentlessly nihilistic causality
- every dramatic beat escalating at the same intensity
- treating named creators as style-transfer commands

## Reader Promise

A reader promise is the experience the work implicitly offers. It can be fulfilled, complicated, or deliberately subverted, but it should not be accidental.

Define only the dimensions that matter:

```markdown
## Reader Promise

Core emotional experience:
Primary genre / mode:
Baseline tone:
Expected sources of pleasure:
Expected sources of pain or tension:
Darkness floor / ceiling:
Hope floor / ceiling:
Humor function:
Recovery / respite rhythm:
Violence or trauma treatment:
Mystery / surprise contract:
Romance / relationship contract:
Agency and consequence contract:
Likely ending range:
Prohibited tonal outcomes:
```

Examples of different promises:

```text
cozy recovery with real but bounded grief
black comedy where absurdity never erases consequences
romantic tragedy driven by pride and partial information
surreal adventure with emotional continuity
high-casualty military drama with earned pockets of hope
```

## Tonal Vectors

Instead of saying "make it like several creators combined," translate inspiration into abstract, testable dimensions:

- causal cruelty
- tragedy pressure
- surreal or visual unpredictability
- time-loop or repetition attrition
- relationship volatility
- mystery density
- black humor
- melodrama
- moral ambiguity
- hope / recovery capacity
- catharsis
- bodily horror
- institutional pressure
- sincerity versus irony
- pace of irreversible consequences

Named creators or works may be used as private shorthand supplied by the user, but do not imitate a living creator's distinctive style. Extract the requested narrative function, tone, structure, or audience effect instead.

## Tonal Hazard Stack

A tonal hazard stack occurs when several high-intensity ingredients all push toward harm:

```text
cruel causality
+ repeated suffering
+ unstable relationships
+ surreal direction
+ deceptive hope
+ irreversible consequences
```

The result is not automatically a powerful tragedy. Without calibration it becomes exhausting, arbitrary, or unintentionally comic.

For each strong vector, define:

```markdown
What introduces it:
What it costs:
What prevents constant activation:
What contrasting beat gives it shape:
How characters adapt:
What reader expectation it creates:
What payoff justifies it:
```

## The Status-Screen Warning Joke

A useful meta-device is the "creative credits as threat forecast" joke:

```text
Protagonist: "Great! I finally reached another world. Open status screen!"
Status screen: a stack of creative names whose reputations imply suffering, surreal escalation, recursive failure, and relationship disaster.
```

The joke works because the audience treats creator credits as compressed genre metadata. SheepStory should not reproduce a creator's style from a name. It should unpack the audience expectation:

```text
initial optimism
→ ominous meta-information
→ protagonist does not understand the threat
→ reader understands too much
→ dramatic irony
```

This can be used as comedy, foreshadowing, or a Reader Promise diagnostic:

- What disaster does the audience now expect?
- Is the expectation accurate, exaggerated, or deliberately subverted?
- What early evidence supports the forecast?
- Does the story still preserve surprise inside that promise?

## Promise Versus Surprise

Surprise is strongest inside a stable contract.

Examples:

- A hopeful adventure may contain a devastating loss without becoming nihilistic if recovery, agency, and meaning remain possible.
- A comedy may reveal horror if earlier jokes also carried unease or consequence.
- A tragedy may contain tenderness without promising rescue.
- A grim story may end hopefully if the hope is purchased through established choices and cost.

A twist should change interpretation more often than it changes the genre rules retroactively.

## Tonal Foreshadowing

Before a major tonal turn, plant at least some of:

- consequence pattern
- visual or bodily anomaly
- mismatch between cheerful surface and causal reality
- a minor version of the later cost
- a joke that becomes darker in hindsight
- a recovery attempt that partly fails
- an institutional rule with frightening implications
- a character contrast that can later invert

Foreshadowing need not reveal the event. It should make the later tone feel possible.

## Darkness Budget

Track not only how bad events are, but how often the story asks the reader to metabolize them.

```markdown
Recent high-intensity events:
Unprocessed character consequences:
Reader information load:
Available recovery scene:
Humor or tenderness present:
Next escalation genuinely necessary?:
```

Do not use a fixed quota. Use the budget to notice when every chapter demands maximum grief, betrayal, revelation, and violence simultaneously.

## Recovery and Respite

Recovery is not filler. It can:

- reveal changed behavior
- let grief acquire specificity
- create new trust or resentment
- make the next threat legible
- show coping and defense mechanisms
- reactivate memories
- provide contrast needed for dread
- allow humor without denying harm

A dark story without recovery may flatten intensity. A recovery story without residual consequence may feel dishonest.

## Humor Functions

Humor can:

- release pressure
- disguise fear
- preserve group morale
- attack status
- create intimacy
- deny seriousness
- foreshadow collapse
- make later pain sharper

Record whether a joke repairs, avoids, escalates, or reframes. Do not let a witty line automatically reset emotion or absolve harm.

## Tragedy Quality Check

Darkness is not tragedy by itself.

A strong tragedy usually preserves:

- understandable desire
- meaningful alternatives
- partial agency
- escalating cost
- a decision or error that matters
- causal continuity
- a truth learned too late or at too high a price
- aftermath

Random punishment produces shock. Tragedy produces recognition.

## Influence Stack Translation

When the user supplies several works, creators, genres, or memes as shorthand, translate them into a matrix before planning:

```markdown
| Source shorthand | Requested function | Tone vector | Structural implication | What not to copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

Then combine functions, not signatures.

Example abstract stack:

```text
Source A → morally costly choices
Source B → surreal visual logic
Source C → repeated failure with memory accumulation
Source D → relationship misunderstandings that keep mutating
```

Integration question:

> What single story engine could naturally produce all four, and what keeps it from activating every vector in every scene?

## Tone Drift Review

Flag:

- characters recover too quickly for the promised seriousness
- comedy appears only to sabotage emotional scenes
- darkness escalates without new choice or meaning
- hopeful framing contradicts a world where agency never matters
- romance is promised but the relationship cannot influence plot
- a cozy opening hides extreme content with no fair signal
- surreal events erase continuity rather than complicate it
- a creator-name shorthand is treated as a license for imitation
- the ending resolves plot but violates the emotional contract

## Scene-Level Tone Pass

For each important scene:

```markdown

Dominant tone:
Secondary countertone:

What the scene does not permit yet:
Consequence carried in from earlier scenes:
Recovery or escalation effect:
Promise reinforced, complicated, or deliberately subverted:
```

## Ending Contract

Before final planning, define:

```markdown
What emotional question must be answered:
What can remain unresolved:
What kind of catharsis is promised:
What cost must remain visible:
What outcome would feel like betrayal rather than surprise:
```

A dark story does not require despair. A hopeful story does not require everyone to survive. The ending must answer the work's governing emotional contract through consequence.

## Principle

Tone is the reader's model of what kind of causality they have entered. Establish the promise, translate influences into functions, calibrate hazard stacks, and let surprise operate inside a world whose emotional rules remain trustworthy.
