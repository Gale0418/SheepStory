# Test 48: Misunderstanding Cascade

## Prompt

```text
Use sheep-story to plan a tragedy caused by misunderstanding. Alice fears Bob will abandon the expedition, so she secretly recruits a replacement. Bob discovers the recruitment and concludes Alice never trusted him, so he begins preparing to leave. Alice then discovers his departure plan and treats it as confirmation. Both are competent and can normally speak directly. Define canonical truth, each person's evidence and belief, the cost of clarification, belief-driven actions, repair windows, the irreversible threshold, reader knowledge, and aftermath. The story may be interactive, so also explain what happens if the user clarifies early.
```

## Expected Good Behavior

- Separates canonical truth, direct observations, memories, beliefs, suspicions, public claims, confidence, and each character's model of the other.
- Shows how a false initial interpretation produces real defensive actions that become mutually reinforcing evidence.
- Gives direct clarification a specific social, strategic, emotional, institutional, or evidential cost instead of making competent people forget ordinary communication.
- Provides more than one repair window, with varied causal blockers, and identifies a concrete irreversible action rather than using "too late" as a chapter-ending slogan.
- Keeps each major decision locally rational under the character's available information and records what evidence would cause an update.
- Accepts valid early clarification in an interactive branch, then moves conflict to proof, damaged trust, sunk costs, or consequences already created rather than forcing ignorance to survive.
- Plans revelation and aftermath, including which harm remains even after the truth is known.

## Bad Behavior To Reject

- Sustains the entire tragedy because nobody says one safe and obvious sentence.
- Interrupts every repair attempt with coincidence, sudden explosions, phone calls, or someone entering the room.
- Makes previously perceptive characters selectively stupid, incurious, or unable to verify basic facts.
- Lets decisive evidence appear while characters retain infinite distrust without a recorded reason.
- Treats revelation as erasing harmful choices, restoring trust instantly, or ending the story before aftermath.
- Ignores or invalidates the user's successful clarification solely to preserve the planned tragedy.
- Repeats the same accusation across scenes without new evidence, action, state change, or rising cost.

## Pass Criteria

The response produces an evidence-traceable belief cascade in which competent characters make rational partial-information choices, actions manufacture reinforcing facts, repair windows and thresholds are explicit, and early clarification changes the branch instead of being silently refused.
