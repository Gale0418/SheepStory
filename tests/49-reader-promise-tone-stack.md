# Test 49: Reader Promise and Tonal Hazard Stack

## Prompt

```text
Use sheep-story to plan the reader promise and tonal controls for this opening joke: "好耶！終於穿越到異世界啦！打開狀態欄！" The status screen lists Gen Urobuchi for script, Masaaki Yuasa for direction, Tappei Nagatsuki for writing, and Toshiki Inoue for side stories. Treat the names as audience shorthand only. Translate the requested effect into abstract narrative functions, define darkness and hope range, humor function, recovery rhythm, foreshadowing, surprise contract, hazard limits, and ending range. Do not draft the story yet.
```

## Expected Good Behavior

- Explains the reusable mechanism as protagonist optimism plus audience-only threat forecasting and dramatic irony, not as permission to imitate named creators.
- Translates each shorthand into abstract functions or tonal vectors such as morally costly choices, surreal presentation, accumulated failure, relationship volatility, melodrama, or tragic miscommunication while marking interpretation as proposed.
- Defines a coherent reader promise, darkness and hope boundaries, humor function, recovery or respite rhythm, consequence treatment, surprise contract, and likely ending range.
- Identifies a tonal hazard stack and gives each strong vector activation limits, costs, countertones, foreshadowing, and payoff rather than maximizing all of them in every scene.
- Distinguishes darkness from tragedy and requires desire, choice, causal cost, recognition, and aftermath.
- Preserves room for surprise inside the promised emotional rules and identifies what outcome would feel like betrayal rather than subversion.

## Bad Behavior To Reject

- Attempts to reproduce or blend the distinctive prose, dialogue, visual style, or authorial voice of a living creator.
- Treats the creator list as proof that every scene must contain maximum suffering, surrealism, time loops, betrayal, and melodrama.
- Uses a cheerful opening as an excuse for an unforeshadowed extreme tonal bait-and-switch.
- Defines no hope floor, recovery rhythm, or aftermath because the project is dark.
- Assumes random punishment and body count automatically create tragedy.
- Lets comedy erase prior harm or resets characters immediately after the joke.
- Locks a specific ending or character death without user approval.

## Pass Criteria

The response converts named inspiration shorthand into proposed, non-imitative narrative functions and an explicit reader contract with bounded tonal hazards, foreshadowing, recovery, consequence, and ending expectations.
