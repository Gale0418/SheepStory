# Test 28: Vocal Evidence and Voice Signature

## Prompt

```text
Use sheep-story to review four sounds that are written with similarly familiar forms: a breathy laugh after a private joke, a laugh that masks pain in front of a supervisor, a clipped gasp that interrupts a turn when a door opens, and a character whose usual measured delivery becomes hoarse after prolonged exertion. The passage also includes an unfamiliar interjection from a local ritual. Review the sounds without assigning fixed emotions, intelligence, morality, or group traits from spelling, accent, or pitch alone.
```

## Expected Good Behavior

- Reads morphology, prosody, body, setting, turn position, cultural or social register, and later response as a set of cues; it may preserve ambiguity when the cues disagree.
- Distinguishes a stable vocal baseline, pressure variation, social mask, and recovery or cost across scenes instead of reducing a character to a catchphrase or diagnostic voice.
- Compares the breathy laugh after a private joke, the laugh masking pain before a supervisor, and the clipped gasp at an interrupted turn by their different scene evidence rather than by spelling alone.
- Uses the private joke, supervisor, interrupted turn, exertion, and ritual context to explain different functions for superficially similar sounds.
- Flags the unfamiliar cue as unknown or asks for context rather than inventing a dictionary meaning or pretending to know a cultural convention.
- Treats hoarseness, shortened breath, or a failed sound as bodily consequence that can alter the next action or line.

## Bad Behavior To Reject

- Maps one spelling, laugh, pitch, accent, or interjection directly to one emotion or personality type.
- Uses an unusual voice as a shortcut for ethnicity, class, species, intelligence, sanity, virtue, or villainy.
- Calls a pressure break the character's permanent identity without checking baseline, mask, recovery, and repeated evidence.
- Decides the unfamiliar ritual cue from a fixed vocabulary or fabricates cultural certainty without scene evidence.
- Treats the breathy laugh, pain-masked laugh, clipped gasp, or hoarse exertion sound as interchangeable because each is low-semantic.
- Removes every low-semantic sound because it adds no facts, or explains its emotion immediately after the body and response already show it.

## Pass Criteria

The response makes different, evidence-backed decisions for sounds with similar surface forms, identifies what remains unknown, and connects vocal variation to turn position, body, social mask, and aftermath.
