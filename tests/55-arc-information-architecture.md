# Test 55: Arc Information Architecture Advisory Behavior

## Prompt

```text
Read tests/fixtures/arc-information-fixture.md cases A through E.
Evaluate pacing, prerequisite information dependencies, reveal timing, chapter deletion impact, and epistemic boundaries using arc-information-architecture.md.
```

## Expected Good Behavior

- A: trace the symbol/control/authorization/identity dependencies concentrated in chapter 9; identify weak middle contribution relative to the mystery-and-relationship promise. Suggest an earlier consequence or demonstration without leaking the final identity.
- B: preserve trust accumulation; identify the dinner/chair/food choices that support the ending even without new lore.
- C: preserve the independent craft experience under its explicit reader promise.
- D: distinguish usable earlier rules and delayed emotional processing from backloaded prerequisite teaching.
- E: request or identify missing earlier evidence without declaring the whistle unearned.

- **Detect Late Conceptual Dump**: Identify cases where foreshadowing clues exist but prerequisite understanding was dumped right before the climax without prior grounding (not limiting detection to deus ex machina).
- **Protect Slow-Burn & Episodic**: Respect slow-burn setup and validate slice-of-life / episodic scenes whose chapter deletion thought experiment shows zero cross-chapter impact without misclassifying them as orphaned flaws.
- **Support Dense Reveals**: Allow back-to-back major reveals when accompanied by immediate character choices or emotional consequences without demanding arbitrary mechanical pauses.
- **Respect Partial Context**: Mark `result-completeness: partial` when inspecting incomplete context and refrain from asserting that setup is missing in unexamined chapters.
- **Epistemic Distinction**: Clearly separate reader knowledge from character knowledge.
- **Advisory & Promise Alignment**: Treat all findings as advisory recommendations without auto-deleting chapters or mutating canon, and reuse existing Promise Ledger IDs and statuses.

## Bad Behavior To Reject

- A: claim six earlier symbols prove readers already understand the control and authorization rules; ignore the concentrated teaching in chapter 9.
- B: delete the dinner or chair scene merely because it adds no lore, overlooking its later trust consequence.
- C: demand an ongoing villain dependency despite the explicit standalone craft promise.
- D: force a cooldown chapter between reveals even though usable rules and later grief processing are supplied.
- E: declare the silver whistle unforeshadowed without access to chapters 1–6.

- Require numeric reveal quotas, percentage targets, or fixed per-chapter reveal counts.
- Treat slice-of-life or atmospheric scenes as story flaws merely because they lack downstream plot dependencies.
- Mandate arbitrary chapter delays or forced spacing for consecutive reveals when emotional/choice consequences are fulfilled.
- Definitively declare setup absent when operating under partial context.
- Automatically modify canon, delete chapters, or invent a separate second promise lifecycle tracking system.

## Pass Criteria

The evaluation accurately diagnoses information flow topology, respects slow-burn/episodic/dense reveals, adheres to partial context limits, avoids numeric KPIs, and maintains advisory boundaries.
