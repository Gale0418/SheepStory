# Ending Outcome Case Map

> Spoiler-heavy research note. This file is for analytical examples, not runtime canon or a requirement to imitate any work.

## Purpose

This note tests the SheepStory Ending Outcome Model against several well-known anime / science-fiction endings that look misleading when reduced to `happy` or `bad`.

The key dimensions are:

```text
Outcome Vector
+ Inherited vs Story-Incurred Cost
+ Recovery
+ Future Possibility
```

A title can succeed at world scale and fail at romance scale. A setting can inherit apocalyptic damage while the main story itself ends relatively gently.

## Aldnoah.Zero

### Observed ending state

- Earth and Vers eventually establish peace and trade.
- Asseylum takes political control and marries Klancain for the political settlement.
- Inaho survives; his artificial eye is removed after the war.
- Slaine survives but is secretly imprisoned while publicly treated as dead / scapegoated.
- The Inaho–Asseylum romantic direction does not become the final couple outcome.

Sources:

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldnoah.Zero
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Aldnoah.Zero_episodes

### Outcome vector

```text
World / war outcome:        strong success
Political peace:            success
Inaho survival:             success
Asseylum survival / agency: success
Slaine personal freedom:    failure
Inaho–Asseylum romance:     failure / non-fulfillment
Future possibility:         high
```

### Cost diagnosis

Calling this simply `low-cost happy ending` depends on scope.

For the central surviving leads, terminal personal cost is relatively bounded compared with many war stories: they survive and retain viable futures. But the series itself contains large-scale war, deaths, destruction, Inaho's serious injuries, and Slaine's imprisonment.

So the better classification is:

```text
world hope: high
central-lead survival: high
romantic fulfillment: low
story-incurred war cost: moderate-high
terminal protagonist cost: low-moderate
recovery / future: high
```

Lesson: `the intended pair did not get together` is not sufficient evidence for a bad ending, and `the main trio lived` is not sufficient evidence for a low-cost war ending.

## Muv-Luv Alternative

### Observed ending state

- Operation Ouka / Cherry Blossom destroys the Original Hive and the Superior.
- The BETA threat is not erased from the universe; humanity receives a much stronger chance to continue the war rather than an instant total cure.
- Multiple core Valkyries die during the final operation; Meiya and Sumika are among the major losses.
- Takeru ceases to remain in the Alternative world and ends in the Final Extra-like world, with the Alternative experience not retained as ordinary explicit memory.
- The work has one main ending; the dead characters cannot simply be saved by route choice.

Sources:

- https://muvluv.fandom.com/wiki/Muv-Luv_Alternative
- https://muvluv.fandom.com/wiki/Mitsurugi_Meiya
- https://muvluv.fandom.com/wiki/Yashiro_Kasumi
- https://w.atwiki.jp/muvluv/pages/14.html

### Outcome vector

```text
Immediate strategic mission:   success
Humanity's future chance:       strong improvement / partial success
BETA extermination:             not achieved
Core squad survival:            catastrophic failure
Takeru personal continuity:     transformed / displaced
Takeru–Sumika ordinary future:  lost in the Alternative world
Future possibility:             preserved
```

### Classification

This is not well described by `everyone died, therefore tragedy`.

It is closer to:

```text
extreme story-incurred cost
+ sacrificial strategic victory
+ partial world hope
+ unusual / displaced personal restoration
```

Depending on which domain the reader weights most, it can feel devastating, hopeful, or both.

A useful compact label is:

> **extreme-cost hopeful / sacrificial victory with transformative restoration**

Lesson: body count and tragedy are not identical. The governing mission can succeed while the cast pays an almost unbearable price.

## Knights of Sidonia

### Observed ending state

The setting begins after enormous inherited catastrophe: humanity is already scattered and under existential threat.

During the ending movement, Tsumugi sacrifices herself to save Nagate, but the manga ultimately restores her; Nagate and Tsumugi live together and have a daughter, while Sidonia survives its conflict.

Sources:

- https://sidonia-no-kishi.fandom.com/wiki/Nagate_Tanikaze
- https://sidonia-no-kishi.fandom.com/wiki/Tsumugi_Shiraui

### Outcome vector

```text
Civilization survival:      success
Main mission:               success
Nagate survival:            success
Nagate–Tsumugi relationship: success
Tsumugi terminal fate:      restored
Future family / continuity: high
```

### Cost diagnosis

This is a useful demonstration of why background apocalypse must not be charged to the finale as if it happened during the evaluated story.

```text
Inherited / background cost: enormous
Story-incurred cost:         meaningful, with real deaths and danger
Terminal recovery:           unusually high for the central relationship
Final future possibility:    very high
```

So the series can inhabit a brutally damaged universe while its central ending is comparatively restorative and generous.

Lesson:

> Grim worldbuilding does not force a grim ending.

## Guilty Crown

### Observed ending state

- Shu stops the Fourth Apocalypse and the Apocalypse Virus is eradicated.
- Inori sacrifices herself to save Shu.
- Shu survives but is blind and uses a prosthetic right arm in the epilogue.
- Tokyo is rebuilt and surviving friends continue living.

Sources:

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilty_Crown
- https://guiltycrown.fandom.com/wiki/Episode_22

### Outcome vector

```text
World / apocalypse outcome: success
Shu survival:              success
Inori survival:            failure
Romantic shared future:    failure
Physical recovery:         partial
Community recovery:        strong
Future possibility:        high
```

### Classification

This belongs in the same broad `high-cost hope succeeds` quadrant as many bittersweet heroic epics, but its recovery distribution is highly personal:

```text
world recovery: high
community recovery: high
romance recovery: none
Shu physical recovery: partial
future possibility: high
```

A useful label is:

> **high-cost bittersweet / sacrificial hopeful ending**

Lesson: two stories can occupy the same Hope × Cost quadrant and still feel very different because the cost lands on different outcome domains.

## Gundam as a Franchise: Why `Happy` Is Misleading

`Gundam` is too broad to classify with one ending label.

A recurring franchise tendency is not exactly `cheerful ending`, but:

> war causes severe loss, yet some political, social, generational, or personal future remains possible.

Counterexamples are important.

### Zeta Gundam (TV)

The Titans are defeated, but the AEUG suffers severe losses and Kamille is mentally devastated by the final battle; Axis remains a major threat.

Sources:

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Suit_Zeta_Gundam

Compact vector:

```text
mission against Titans: success
Kamille personal recovery at cutoff: very low
collective cost: high
future conflict: unresolved
```

This is much harsher than a conventional happy ending.

### Iron-Blooded Orphans

Tekkadan loses its power struggle; Orga, Mikazuki, Akihiro and others die, but survivors escape, Mars gains a better political future, and reforms occur after the defeat.

Source:

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Suit_Gundam:_Iron-Blooded_Orphans

Compact vector:

```text
Tekkadan institutional goal: failure
core protagonist survival: failure
survivors' future: success
Mars political future: improvement
cost: extreme
```

This is a mixed tragedy / sacrificial legacy outcome rather than simple total defeat.

### The Witch from Mercury

The final state is much more restorative: Suletta and Miorine survive, build a shared future, and the epilogue confirms their marriage; substantial earlier harm remains part of the story.

Sources:

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Suit_Gundam:_The_Witch_from_Mercury
- https://en.gundam-official.com/witch-from-mercury/story/18/

This shows how much Gundam ending tone can vary between installments.

### Franchise lesson

Do not model `Gundam = tragic` or `Gundam = happy`.

Model the actual work:

```text
war cost
+ protagonist outcome
+ political outcome
+ relationship outcome
+ generational future
+ recovery
```

## Research Lessons for SheepStory

1. **Hope is a vector, not one number.**
2. **Relationship failure is not world failure.**
3. **Protagonist survival is not proof of low cost.**
4. **Inherited apocalypse is not the same as story-incurred ending cost.**
5. **Recovery distribution matters as much as the quadrant.**
6. **A cast can suffer extreme casualties while the governing mission still succeeds.**
7. **A grim franchise can repeatedly end with future possibility without being `cheerful`.**
8. **The same Hope × Cost quadrant can contain very different emotional endings depending on who paid and what recovered.**

## Principle

Do not ask only whether the ending is happy.

Ask:

> Happy for whom, successful at what scale, paid by whom, compared with what starting damage, recovered how far, and what future is still possible?
